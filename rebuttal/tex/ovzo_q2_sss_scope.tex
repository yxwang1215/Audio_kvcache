\begin{answer}
    \paragraph{Response to Reviewer ovZo-Q2.}
    We thank the reviewer for this important suggestion.
    We agree that the temporal-continuity motivation of SSS is most directly justified for audio tokens, whereas applying the same smoothing to instruction/prompt tokens and generated text tokens is less well motivated.
    To verify the effective scope of SSS, we conducted an \emph{audio-only SSS} ablation in which the RFFT-based smoothing is applied only to the detected audio-token span, while all non-audio tokens keep their original unsmoothed importance scores.
    We compare this variant against the original \emph{full-history SSS}, with the model, head scores, SparseKV setting, budget, and decoding configuration fixed.

    \paragraph{Controlled setup.}
    Both variants use Qwen2.5-Omni-3B with retention budget $\rho=0.8$, the same audio-aware head scores, and the same SSS hyperparameters (low-pass cutoff ratio $0.7$ and interpolation weight $\alpha=0.5$).
    The only changed factor is the token scope of the smoothing operator:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Full-history SSS}: smooth the entire historical importance sequence.
        \item \textbf{Audio-only SSS}: smooth only the audio-token segment and leave textual-token scores unchanged.
    \end{itemize}

    \paragraph{Findings.}
    The two variants are nearly identical in performance.
    On MLS Spanish, audio-only SSS slightly improves WER from 6.06 to 5.98, and on MLS French it remains essentially tied with full-history SSS (8.48 vs. 8.45 WER).
    This shows that restricting smoothing to audio tokens preserves almost all of the benefit of the original method.
    Therefore, the gain is primarily attributable to modeling acoustic temporal continuity, rather than to smoothing textual tokens in the prompt or generated response.

    \paragraph{Revision plan.}
    In the revision, we will add Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal-ovzo-q2-sss-scope} and clarify that the main effect of SSS comes from the audio-token region.
    We will correspondingly moderate the description of full-history smoothing, presenting it as an implementation choice whose benefit is comparable to the cleaner audio-only variant, rather than as evidence that textual tokens should also satisfy the same continuity assumption.
