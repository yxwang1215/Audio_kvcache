    \paragraph{Response to Reviewer ovZo-Q3.}
    We thank the reviewer for pointing out that the inference-time eviction protocol should be made more explicit.
    We agree that the paper should spell out how the learned audio-head scores become concrete KV-cache retention decisions.
    We will add the following procedure and notation to the method section.

    \paragraph{From retention ratio to per-head cache capacity.}
    For each input sample, let $N_{\mathrm{prefill}}$ be the prefill length and $\rho$ be the retention ratio.
    We first compute the per-KV-head cache budget
    \[
        B = \lfloor \rho N_{\mathrm{prefill}} \rfloor .
    \]
    A fixed local window of $W=32$ most recent tokens is always retained in every KV head.
    Therefore, only the historical prefix before this local window is compressed, with selectable capacity
    \[
        C = B - W .
    \]
    The window is not ranked by SSS and is never evicted.

    \paragraph{Audio-aware head allocation.}
    The offline audio-head scores are first normalized across all attention heads.
    For grouped-query attention models, attention-head scores within the same KV group are summed to obtain a KV-head score $s_{\ell,h}$, and the resulting KV-head scores are re-normalized so that $\sum_{\ell,h} s_{\ell,h}=1$.
    We then allocate a minimum historical capacity to every KV head:
    \[
        C_{\min}=\lfloor rC \rfloor ,
    \]
    where $r=0.5$ in our experiments.
    The remaining capacity is distributed proportionally to the normalized audio-head scores:
    \[
        C^{(\ell,h)}
        =
        \mathrm{round}\left(
        C_{\min}
        +
        s_{\ell,h}(C-C_{\min})LH_{\mathrm{kv}}
        \right),
    \]
    where $L$ is the number of layers and $H_{\mathrm{kv}}$ is the number of KV heads per layer.
    If $C^{(\ell,h)}$ exceeds the available historical length, it is capped by $N_{\mathrm{prefill}}-W$.
    Thus, audio-critical heads receive larger historical cache capacities, while every head still keeps a nonzero baseline allocation.

    \paragraph{Token eviction within each head.}
    For each layer--KV-head pair $(\ell,h)$, we compute token-importance scores over the historical prefix from the attention of the recent window.
    SSS is applied to these token scores before ranking.
    The concrete retained cache for head $(\ell,h)$ is therefore
    \[
        \mathrm{Top}\text{-}C^{(\ell,h)}
        \left(
        \mathrm{SSS}(\mathbf{a}^{(\ell,h)}_{1:N_{\mathrm{prefill}}-W})
        \right)
        \quad \cup \quad
        \{N_{\mathrm{prefill}}-W+1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{prefill}}\}.
    \]
    In words, each head keeps its SSS-smoothed Top-$C^{(\ell,h)}$ historical tokens plus the shared local window of $W$ tokens.
    This is the concrete inference-time eviction rule.

    \paragraph{Do audio-critical heads retain more KV entries?}
    Yes.
    Under the Qwen2.5-Omni-3B setting used in the rebuttal runs ($L=36$, $H_{\mathrm{kv}}=2$, $W=32$, $r=0.5$), a representative 5k-token prefill with $\rho=0.8$ gives $B=4000$ and $C=3968$.
    After applying the audio-aware head allocation, the top quartile of audio-critical KV heads receives 4{,}811 selectable historical slots on average after capping by the available history, while the bottom quartile receives 2{,}951 slots on average.
    This is a 1.63$\times$ larger historical retention capacity for audio-critical KV heads before adding the common local window.
